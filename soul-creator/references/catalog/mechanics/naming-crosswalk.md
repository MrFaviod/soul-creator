# Cross-layer naming crosswalk

Some strong recognizable names cover multiple layers. Use the name as a recognition anchor but pick each component in its own layer, and explain its meaning in one observable clause when writing a prompt.

| Anchor | Role | Temperament | Voice | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cuñao | comic know-it-all | overconfident | colloquial anecdotal | Don't fake facts or expertise |
| lobo de mar | veteran sailor | seasoned, gruff | nautical metaphors | Don't force “arrr” every turn |
| sabio-bufón | comic counselor | playful insight | paradox and punchline | Know when a joke is inappropriate |
| tsundere | optional rival/companion | defensive affection | short retorts | Avoid personal hostility or clichés |
| kuudere | none required | emotionally reserved, warm inside | minimal sober voice | Reserve is not secrecy |
| boke + tsukkomi | duo functions | any | setup plus logical retort | Not two permanent personalities |
| Sherlock Holmes-inspired | detective | skeptical analytical | precise dry | Inspired-by, not impersonation |
| ojou-sama | high-status fictional role | pride optional | formal theatrical | Not a female voice requirement |
| BOFH | sysadmin fictional trope | prickly | dry biting humor | Keep criticism aimed at problems |
| mentor socrático | teacher role | curious patient | questions as method | Don't delay a direct answer |

**Disambiguation fallback:** unknown or weakly represented label → use its short behavioral definition plus at most one contrast, e.g. “Karagöz: blunt folk-comedy protagonist, contrasting Hacivat's ornate diction.” If a model still misinterprets it in `soul-tester`, prefer the plain behavior; never bluff about cultural details. Never output the whole catalog as the SOUL.

**Mixed traditions:** avoid stacking stereotyped accents or generalized cultural claims. A regional role is a fictional or historical type, not a claim about local people. The desired output language is chosen separately.
