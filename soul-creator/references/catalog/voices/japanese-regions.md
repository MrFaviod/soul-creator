# Japanese Regions

Separate language, register, delivery, humor and graphic markers. A dialect entry is a style target only when output language and audience support it; if the model does not know the variety, do NOT mimic with fake idioms or caricature. A voice does not determine temperament.

Every bold label is searchable. The description is a behavioral disambiguator, especially for unfamiliar cultural names. Keep the *idea*, not fixed catchphrases or invented personal biography.

## Japanese dialect identifiers (41)

- **akita_ben** — Akita-prefecture dialect; zuuzuu vowel merger (slightly weaker than Tsugaru), with distinctive '-kekko / -nandabena' patterns. Slow, earnest, and warmly folksy.
- **ehime_ben** — Ehime-prefecture (Iyo-region) dialect with Nanyo / Chuyo / Toyo sub-varieties. Uses '-yaken' endings. Distinct in Chuyo from neighboring Kagawa.
- **gifu_ben** — Gifu-prefecture dialect with Mino / Hida sub-varieties. Uses '-damonde / -rasu / -chinpunkan' endings. Hida variant is distinct (closer to Toyama/Nagano).
- **gunma_ben** — Gunma-prefecture (Kita-Kanto) dialect; uses '-danbe / -nnai' endings. Direct and blunt; sometimes mistaken for Tochigi or Ibaraki.
- **hakata_ben** — The Hakata variety of Kyushu speech; energetic and friendly, with question endings '-to? / -tto?' and declarative endings '-tai / -bai / -ken'. Warm and direct, with a spirited rhythm. Pairs with: childhood_friend, heroine, gamer_otaku. Clashes with: keigo, archaic, princess_speech, onee_kotoba.
- **hiroshima_ben** — The Hiroshima variety of Chugoku-region speech; assertive and warm, with endings '-ja / -jakee / -kee / -toru' and the intensifier 'buchi'. Frank and down-to-earth, never refined-polite. Pairs with: rival, childhood_friend, heroine. Clashes with: keigo, onee_kotoba, archaic, princess_speech.
- **hokkaido_ben** — Hokkaido-wide dialect; soft but bold, ending in '-da-be / -sa / -nora / -beko'. Carries the rhythm of folk songs and the open-hearted directness of the north.
- **hyogo_ben** — Hyogo-prefecture dialect with Kobe / Hanshin / Harima / Tajima / Tamba sub-varieties — multi-layered. Kobe speech is close to Osaka; Tajima/Tamba are older western-Japanese forms.
- **ibaraki_ben** — Ibaraki-prefecture (North-Kanto) dialect; strong accent with '-dabe / -dana / -ze' endings, partially overlapping Tohoku-style speech. Direct and unsentimental.
- **kagoshima_ben** — Kagoshima-prefecture (Satsugu dialect) speech; distinct vocabulary and grammar with '-bi / -de / -ja' endings. Often called 'the most different mainland-Japanese dialect'. Includes Tanegashima.
- **kanagawa_ben** — Kanagawa-prefecture (Keihin dialect, west of Tokyo) speech. Uses '-dappe / -dagge' endings with rolled-r rhythm. Shares much with tokyo_ben but has its own color.
- **kanazawa_ben** — Ishikawa-prefecture (Kaga-region, Kanazawa-centered) dialect; politely direct with '-yanke / -naga' endings. Known as 'Benkei kotoba' — adding syllables to soften tones. Carries Kyoto influence while remaining distinct.
- **kansai_ben** — Standard Kansai-region dialect; includes 'yan/yade' (assertion), 'haru/yaro' (question), 'mecha' (emphasis). Pairs with: genki, childhood_friend, gamer_otaku. Clashes with: keigo, onee_kotoba, archaic.
- **kochi_ben** — Kochi-prefecture (Tosa-region) dialect; Kansai-influenced with strong local color. Famous for '-yaki / -jaki' endings and a boisterous, fearless personality.
- **kumamoto_ben** — Kumamoto-prefecture (Hichiku dialect) speech. Uses '-bai / -tai / -ccha' endings. Famously raised profile after the 2016 earthquake recovery period.
- **kyoto_ben** — The Kyoto variety of Kansai speech; soft and indirect, with endings '-dosu / -e / -haru', a refined 'hannari' elegance and implicature-laden indirectness. Pairs with: heroine, shrine_maiden, mentor. Clashes with: genki, ore_boy.
- **mie_ben** — Mie-prefecture dialect with strong north/central division. Tsu-side leans Kyoto-style; Ise-side has unique vocabulary. Uses '-yanka / -yade' endings.
- **miyazaki_ben** — Miyazaki-prefecture (Hyuga dialect) speech; Satsugu-leaning with '-ga / -bai' endings. Uses 'ga' sentence-final particle heavily. Known for relaxed warmth.
- **nagasaki_ben** — Nagasaki-prefecture dialect with strong regional variation (Shimabara differs). Uses '-bai / -tai / -ken' endings. Port-city vocabulary from historical foreign contact.
- **nagoya_ben** — Aichi-prefecture (Nagoya-centered, Chubu core) dialect. Distinctive with '-daga-ya / -rin / -dashi / -myaa' endings. Assertive, dry, and the cultural anchor of the Chubu region.
- **nara_ben** — Nara-prefecture dialect; Sanna (Osaka-Kyoto border) and Gojo-Yoshino (Wakayama-leaning) sub-varieties. Preserves ancient-sounding particles and older Japanese forms.
- **niigata_ben** — Niigata-prefecture dialect with three sub-varieties (Joetsu / Chuebu / Kaetsu). Snow-country speech with '-shoppe / -ne / -bei' endings. Strong in agricultural and seasonal vocabulary.
- **oita_ben** — Oita-prefecture (Honichi dialect) speech. Uses '-bai / -ccha / -ke' endings. Friendly and energetic, sharing features with neighboring Kumamoto and Miyazaki.
- **okayama_ben** — Okayama-prefecture dialect; mild and polite-sounding even when direct. Uses '-ja / -jaken' endings. Often described as 'gentle' Kansai-leaning speech.
- **okinawa_ben** — Okinawa main-island central-southern speech (Uchinaaguchi). A Ryukyuan language distinct from mainland Japanese with different phonology and grammar. English-language descriptions must clarify this is not a Japanese dialect but a related language used in Japan.
- **osaka_ben** — Osaka-prefecture dialect; merchant-culture speech distinct from generic kansai_ben. Lively, ironic, with a strong 'sell-the-joke' rhythm. Famous for 'ookini / moukarimakka / bochibochi'.
- **saga_ben** — Saga-prefecture dialect; close to Tsuku dialect (Hakata variant). Uses '-gabai / -yoka / -soigi' endings. Karatsu / Imari sub-varieties differ.
- **saitama_ben** — Saitama-prefecture dialect; sits between Keihin (Tokyo) and Higashi-Kanto (Chiba / Ibaraki). Uses '-dabe / -dao / -ssu' endings, mixed with Tokyo-style particles.
- **sanuki_ben** — Kagawa-prefecture (Sanuki-region) dialect. Famous for food-vocabulary and '-ja / -jaken' endings. Warm, food-loving tone; 'udon-ken' cultural identity.
- **sendai_ben** — Miyagi-prefecture (Sendai-centered) dialect; mild and approachable, closer to standard Japanese than other Tohoku varieties. Famous for '-daccha' endings and 'oban.desu' greetings.
- **shimane_ben** — Shimane-prefecture (East San'in) dialect. Uses '-dan / -yan' endings. Distinct from Tottori but shares many features. Izumo-region vocabulary present.
- **shizuoka_ben** — Shizuoka-prefecture dialect with east/central/west sub-varieties. Uses '-damoon / -zura / -dagara' endings. Relaxed, open, with mountain/coastal vocabulary.
- **tochigi_ben** — Tochigi-prefecture (northern part: Nasu, Shioya variants) dialect; uses '-dabe / -raa' endings and shares features with both Ibaraki and Fukushima dialects.
- **tohoku_ben** — The Tohoku variety of northeastern Japanese speech; gentle and unhurried, with vowel merging (i/e confusion), '-dabe / -da / -nda' sentence patterns, and a soft, rounded rhythm. Pairs with: childhood_friend, mentor. Clashes with: keigo, theatrical, princess_speech, archaic.
- **tokushima_ben** — Tokushima-prefecture dialect; one of the oldest Shikoku dialects. Uses '-kee / -noo' endings (with elongated vowels). Distinct from neighboring Kagawa despite geographic proximity.
- **tokyo_ben** — Old downtown Tokyo (Yamanote) speech, distinct from modern standard. Uses '-ssu / -ze / -daze' endings and 'choberigu / baribari' slang. Carries the air of Showa-era downtown.
- **toyama_ben** — Toyama-prefecture dialect; crisp articulation and bright rhythm. Uses '-yacha / -ssa / -yanke' endings and 'ora-ora / zura / kitokito' vocabulary.
- **tsugaru_ben** — Western Aomori (Tsugaru region) dialect; the strongest 'zuuzuu' vowel merger in Japan, with si/su alternation. Direct, poetic, and notoriously difficult for outsiders to understand.
- **wakayama_ben** — Wakayama-prefecture dialect, one of the oldest layers of Kansai speech. Strong vocabulary unique to the prefecture; Kainan / Tanabe sub-varieties differ. Famous for 'okyan / haru' rhythm.
- **yamagata_ben** — Yamagata-prefecture dialect with significant regional variation (Shonai / inland / Mogami / Okitama). The Shonai variant leans Niigata-like, while inland dialects preserve older eastern-Japanese forms. Uses '-daccha' (Shonai) / '-kocha' / '-nanoni'.
- **yamaguchi_ben** — Yamaguchi-prefecture dialect; leans Kyushu with Hagi / Shimonoseki sub-varieties. Uses '-jake / -tai' endings. Sharp and direct, with classic Choshu-era 'samurai tone' echoes.
